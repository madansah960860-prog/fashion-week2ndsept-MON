Materials and impact

This page exists to be checked. Every claim below is either a certification we hold, a number we can document, or a limitation we are stating plainly. Where we do not have data, we say so rather than filling the gap with adjectives.

The first thing that matters is durability

The most sustainable garment is the one you do not replace.

Roughly 60% of the environmental cost of a piece of apparel is locked in before it reaches you, in fiber production, dyeing and finishing. That means the single largest lever available to us is making things last, because doubling a garment's useful life roughly halves its impact per year of wear. Everything else on this page is secondary to that.

So our first commitment is unglamorous: fifty wash cycles of testing before a fabric is approved, patterns graded across the full size range so garments are not stressed at the extremes, and a repair program that keeps things in use.

What our fabrics are

Recycled synthetics

  • Recycled polyester and nylon make up about 71% of our fabric volume by weight
  • Feedstock is post-consumer PET and post-industrial nylon waste, certified to the Global Recycled Standard
  • Recycled polyester uses roughly 30 to 50% less energy than virgin polyester, depending on the mill
  • Limitation: recycled synthetics still shed microfibers in the wash. A filter bag or a washing machine filter is the only real mitigation today

Wool

  • All merino is certified to the Responsible Wool Standard, covering animal welfare and land management
  • Sourced from grower groups in New Zealand and Australia
  • Micron counts are published on each product: 17.5 for the Featherweight Tee, 18.5 for the Ridge Half-Zip
  • Limitation: wool has a higher land and water footprint than recycled synthetics. We use it where its performance and longevity justify that

Cotton and cellulosics

  • All cotton is certified organic, grown without synthetic pesticides or GM seed
  • Tencel lyocell comes from FSC certified wood in a closed-loop solvent process that recovers over 99% of the solvent
  • Used in the Loop Terry Crewneck, Recovery Sweatpant and blended into the Session Tee
  • Limitation: organic cotton is not low-water. It is lower-chemical

Chemistry and finishes

  • Every water-repellent finish in the line is PFAS-free, completed across the range in 2024
  • Membranes are polyurethane rather than PTFE
  • Eleven of our fabrics come from bluesign approved mills
  • Limitation: PFAS-free finishes need reproofing more often. That is a real trade-off and we would rather you knew about it

Where things are made

We work with two cut-and-sew partners and one knitting mill. We are a small customer at each, which limits our leverage, and we think it is more useful to say that than to imply we set their standards.

Manufacturing partners

  • Outerwear and technical wovens: a family-owned factory in Da Nang, Vietnam, WRAP certified, audited annually by an independent third party
  • Knitwear: a knitting and finishing mill in Porto, Portugal, operating under EU labor law with SA8000 certification
  • Bench Made: our own workshop in Portland, Oregon, where five made-to-order styles are cut and sewn
  • Audits: summaries are available on request by email. We will send the full report, not a summary of a summary

Freight

  • All bulk production moves by sea, never air, even when it delays a launch
  • Drop 06 shipped two weeks later than planned for exactly this reason
  • US parcel delivery is ground service by default. Expedited is available and we do not push it

Repair, resale and end of life

Repairs

  • Zip replacement, seam repair and patching on any Splitsec garment
  • Charged at parts cost plus return shipping, typically $18 to $45
  • Turnaround is two to three weeks from our Portland workshop
  • Manufacturing faults are repaired or replaced free, no time limit

Resale

  • We take back worn Splitsec pieces in usable condition for store credit
  • Credit is 20 to 35% of the original price, depending on condition
  • Returned pieces are cleaned, repaired and resold at a reduced price
  • Email us before shipping anything back so we can confirm the credit

End of life

  • Genuinely finished garments can be sent back for textile recycling
  • We work with a regional textile recycler rather than shipping waste overseas
  • Mixed-fiber garments are downcycled into insulation and industrial felt
  • Limitation: true fiber-to-fiber recycling of blends is not commercially viable yet. We will not pretend otherwise

Claims we do not make

Environmental marketing has a credibility problem, and most of it comes from claims that sound absolute but mean nothing specific. Here is what we will not say about ourselves.

  • We are not carbon neutral. We have not completed a full scope 1 to 3 footprint, so any offset claim would be arithmetic on a guess
  • We are not zero waste. Cutting waste on wovens runs around 12 to 15%. We sell what we can as offcuts and are working the number down
  • We are not plastic free. Most technical performance fabric is plastic. Polybags are still used in shipping from our factories, though our own outbound packaging is paper
  • We do not call anything eco or green. Those words have no agreed definition, so they carry no information
  • We do not claim a percentage improvement without a baseline. If we cannot show the before and the after, we do not publish the number

Ask us for the documentation

Certification numbers, mill test reports and audit summaries are available by email to anyone who asks, including competitors and journalists. If a claim on this site cannot be backed by a document, tell us and we will correct the page.
